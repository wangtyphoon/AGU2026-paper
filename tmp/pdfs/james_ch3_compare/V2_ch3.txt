 407 
The predictability-filtered MSE objective is designed to retain accuracy at predictable scales 408 
while reducing the incentive to excessively damp spectral variability. The spectral-error loss 409 
constrains the distribution of variability across scales, and the spectral-bias penalty limits 410 
systematic drift. Together, these terms are intended to improve the physical consistency of 411 
autoregressive rollouts. 412 
 413 
We compare the MSE-only baseline with two spectrum-aware variants that penalize absolute or 414 
squared spectral errors. For fixed normalization, squared-error gradients increase with error 415 
magnitude, whereas absolute-error gradients remain constant away from zero. However, the 416 
variants differ in both norm and normalization. Their comparison therefore evaluates the 417 
combined formulations. Spectra are computed using a planar Fourier approximation of the 418 
regional domain, with radial wavenumber binning under an assumption of statistical isotropy. 419 
3 Evaluation 420 
All evaluations use the 2024 test set, following the tropical-cyclone-centered sampling criterion 421 
described in Section 2.1. Forecasts are initialized at 00, 06, 12, and 18 UTC. We evaluate three 422 
variants of the proposed model: the Stage 3 checkpoint trained solely with the weighted MSE 423 
objective and two Stage 4 variants incorporating the Spectrum-MAE and Spectrum-MSE 424 
formulations, respectively. 425

Aurora (Bodnar et al., 2025; Lam et al., 2023), have demonstrated improved performance in 429 
conventional global forecast metrics, including RMSE, Pangu-Weather is useful for the 430

Although the nine-grid-point boundary buffer is excluded from verification, prescribed ERA5 441 
boundary forcing can still influence the interior domain during autoregressive integration. 442 
Accuracy metrics from the second forecast step onward therefore characterize a boundary-443 
constrained regional system and do not constitute a comparison with Pangu-Weather under 444 
identical information constraints. Accordingly, Pangu-Weather serves as an external reference, 445 
while the effects of spectrum-aware training are assessed primarily by comparing the three 446 
variants of the proposed model under the same lateral boundary conditions. 447

To characterize the scale dependence of forecast variability, we analyze the Fourier power 469 
spectra of the forecast and reference fields using isotropic radial binning. These diagnostics 470 
identify the spatial scales at which forecast variance is attenuated or amplified relative to ERA5, 471 
complementing forecast activity, which measures overall variability without resolving its 472 
distribution across scales. They show whether mesoscale variability is retained throughout the 473 
forecast but do not directly diagnose energy transfer between scales. 474 
 475 
For TC verification, we adopt a TC tracker similar to that in Aurora (Bodnar et al., 2025), 476 
augmented with a weighted-centroid algorithm. The cyclone center is identified primarily using 477 
the minimum mean sea level pressure (MSLP). Over land, where MSLP-based tracking may 478 
become less reliable, the 700-hPa geopotential-height field is instead used to determine the 479 
cyclone center. Linear extrapolation from the preceding track positions stabilizes center selection 480 
and reduces unrealistic jumps between consecutive forecast times. Only cases satisfying a 481 
minimum TC intensity threshold of 35 kt in the JTWC best-track maximum sustained winds are 482 
retained for verification. 483

4 Synoptic-Scale Metrics 516 
We use specific humidity and meridional wind as representative synoptic-scale variables because 517 
they characterize TC moisture distributions and wind fields. To assess these aspects of TC 518 
structure and environment, we supplement RMSE and ACC with forecast bias and activity. 519 
These complementary metrics help distinguish retained atmospheric variability from 520 
improvements that may primarily reflect calibration. Here, intrinsic skill refers to the model’s 521 
ability to reproduce the observed atmospheric evolution and variability, whereas calibration 522 
denotes improvements that can be achieved through procedures such as smoothing, filtering, or 523 
systematic bias correction (Bonavita & Geer, 2026). 524 
 525 
As shown in Figure 3, after the prescribed lateral boundary conditions begin to influence the 526 
forecasts, our model exhibits RMSE values approximately 10% lower than those of Pangu-527 
Weather, indicating comparable or higher pointwise accuracy under the present evaluation 528 
framework. This advantage initially decreases with forecast lead time and subsequently increases 529 
again, possibly because the influence of the lateral boundary conditions gradually propagates 530 
into the interior of the regional domain. Comparing the different training objectives, both 531 
Spectrum-MAE and Spectrum-MSE generally exhibit higher RMSE than the MSE-trained 532 
model, whereas their effects on ACC are relatively moderate. For specific humidity, both Pangu-533 
Weather and the MSE-trained model show pronounced dry biases at longer lead times, 534 
suggesting that their moisture distributions become progressively less consistent with the 535 
reanalysis field and limiting the fidelity of their moisture forecasts at longer lead times. 536
